# Home Practice Kit — Assembly Guide (for the coach)

Assemble one kit per student. First time takes about an hour; after that it's just
refills. Hand out at Session 1, along with the
[Home Lab Safety Agreement](Home_Lab_Safety_Agreement.md).

## Shopping list (makes ~4 kits)

**Powders — all from a grocery store:**
- Baking soda
- Corn starch
- Table salt (iodized is fine)
- Granulated sugar
- Powdered (confectioner's) sugar
- Epsom salt (pharmacy — plain, unscented)
- Cream of tartar (spice aisle)
- Citric acid (canning aisle, or "sour salt")
- Calcium carbonate — sidewalk chalk crushed to powder, or a plain calcium
  supplement crushed, or "garden lime"
- Play sand (a small bag; rinse and dry it first)
- *(optional extras for later: baking powder, unflavored gelatin, MSG,
  sodium-acetate hand-warmer beads, washing soda / "super washing soda")*

**Reagents:**
- Tincture of iodine (pharmacy antiseptic — the 2% "tincture," not povidone/
  Betadine, and not "decolorized" iodine). One small bottle per kit.
- Red cabbage (one head makes indicator for the whole team, several times).

**Containers:**
- Small zip bags (snack size) or mini condiment cups with lids — ~11 per kit
- One 1-oz dropper bottle per kit for the iodine
- One 2-oz bottle per kit for the cabbage indicator
- A sandwich-size bag or small box to hold each kit
- Sticky labels

## Portioning the powders

Into each labeled bag/cup, put about **2–3 tablespoons**. That's plenty for many
practice runs. Label each with the **name** (the kids already know them — this
isn't a blind set; blind "unknown" sets are made fresh at practice).

Label set: `baking soda · corn starch · table salt · granulated sugar · powdered
sugar · Epsom salt · cream of tartar · citric acid · calcium carbonate · sand`
plus a couple of **blank numbered bags** ("Unknown 1", "Unknown 2") the parent
can fill for a blind quiz.

## Making red-cabbage indicator

1. Chop about ¼ of a red cabbage.
2. Cover with water in a pot, simmer 10–15 minutes until the water is deep
   purple. (Or pour boiling water over chopped cabbage in a bowl and wait 30
   min.)
3. Strain out the cabbage. The purple liquid is your indicator.
4. Cool, then fill each kit's 2-oz dropper bottle. Refrigerate the rest; it keeps
   about a week (freeze in an ice tray for longer — thaw a cube as needed).

Color guide to print small and tuck in each kit:

| Color | Approx. pH |
|---|---|
| red | 2 |
| pink | 4 |
| purple | 7 (neutral) |
| blue-purple | 8 |
| blue-green | 9–10 |
| green | 11 |
| yellow | 12+ |

## What the family supplies (put this list in the kit)

- White vinegar
- Water
- A magnet (fridge magnet is fine)
- Clear cups, or a muffin tin / ice-cube tray
- Toothpicks, a spoon
- Paper towels
- Safety goggles or well-fitting glasses (if the family doesn't have goggles, add
  a cheap pair to the kit)

## What is deliberately NOT in the kit

No strong acid or base. No hydrogen peroxide (fine for the family to have, but
not part of the kit — kids shouldn't be doing the blood-catalase demo
unsupervised). No heat source, no flame, nothing for burn tests. Those all stay
at in-person practice.

## Refills

Kids will run low on iodine and cabbage indicator first. Bring a refill bottle of
each to every meeting. Powders last most of the season; top up the ones they use
most (baking soda, corn starch, citric acid).

## Cost

Roughly $8–12 per kit, most of it one-time (the powders and cabbage serve the
whole season). The dropper bottles are the main recurring cost if kids lose them.
