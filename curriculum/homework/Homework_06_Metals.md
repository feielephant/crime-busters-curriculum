## Part B: Density (3 pts each)

1. A metal cube 3.0 cm on a side weighs 72.9 g. Density? Which metal?
2. A piece of metal raises water in a graduated cylinder from 25.0 mL to 34.0 mL
   and weighs 80.6 g. Density? Which metal?
3. Convert 7.14 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
4. A solid iron bar has a volume of 50 cm³. What is its mass? (Fe = 7.87 g/cm³)
5. A 2.0-meter cube of aluminum — what is its mass in kg? (Al = 2700 kg/m³)

## Part C: Reactions & oxides (2 pts each)

1. Classify: `2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂`
2. Classify: `Zn + 2 HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂`
3. Classify: `2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO`
4. Why does aluminum resist acid at first, but iron just keeps corroding?
5. Steel, brass, and bronze — name the metals in each.

## Part D: Scenario (3 pts)

A gray metal fragment at a scene is not attracted to a magnet, has a measured
density of 2.71 g/cm³, and reacts slowly with HCl at first and then steadily.
Name the metal. A suspect who works with aircraft parts and a suspect who works
with cast-iron cookware are both of interest — which does this implicate?

---

## Answer Key

**Part A:** 1. Copper — Cu — 29.  2. Aluminum — Al — 13.  3. Iron — Fe — 26.
4. Magnesium — Mg — 12.  5. Zinc — Zn — 30.

**Part B:**
1. Volume = 27.0 cm³; 72.9 ÷ 27.0 = **2.70 g/cm³ → aluminum**.
2. Volume = 9.0 mL; 80.6 ÷ 9.0 ≈ **8.96 g/cm³ → copper**.
3. **7140 kg/m³**.
4. 7.87 × 50 = **393.5 g**.
5. (2.0)³ = 8.0 m³; 8.0 × 2700 = **21,600 kg**.

**Part C:**
1. Decomposition.  2. Single replacement.  3. Synthesis (combination).
4. Aluminum forms a thin, tough, **protective** oxide layer (Al₂O₃) that seals
   the surface (passivation); iron's oxide (rust, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O) is flaky and does
   not protect, so oxygen keeps reaching fresh metal.
5. Steel = iron + carbon; brass = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin.

**Part D:** Aluminum (non-magnetic, density ~2.70, oxide-layer delay then steady
reaction). It implicates the **aircraft-parts** suspect (aircraft use aluminum
alloys); the cast-iron-cookware suspect is cleared — that would be iron, which is
magnetic and much denser.
