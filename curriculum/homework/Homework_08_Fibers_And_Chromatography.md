## Part A: Burn-test flow chart (2 pts each)

Name the class (animal / vegetable / synthetic) and a likely specific fiber:
1. Shrinks away from the flame before it touches, melts and drips, hard shiny
   bead, sharp chemical smell.
2. Burns steadily with an afterglow, smells like burning paper, soft gray ash,
   no bead.
3. Curls away, sputters, self-extinguishes, smells like singed hair, brittle
   black bead.
4. Smooth and shiny (not scaly), burns slowly, self-extinguishes, smells like
   burning hair.
5. Burns very fast with almost no melting, smells like paper, very little ash.

## Part C: Rf calculations (2 pts each)

1. A dye spot moved 2.4 cm; the solvent front moved 8.0 cm. Rf?
2. A dye spot moved 7.2 cm; the solvent front moved 8.0 cm. Rf? Is this dye more
   or less soluble in the solvent than the dye in #1?
3. A spot stayed exactly on the start line. Rf?
4. Can an Rf ever be greater than 1? Why or why not?

## Part D: Matching a pen (4 pts)

The crime-scene note's ink separates into **3 spots** with Rf values **0.30,
0.55, 0.80**. Three suspects' pens give:
- Pen A: 2 spots at Rf 0.30, 0.80
- Pen B: 3 spots at Rf 0.30, 0.55, 0.80
- Pen C: 3 spots at Rf 0.25, 0.50, 0.75

Which pen wrote the note? Why not the others?

---

## Answer Key

**Part A:** 1. Synthetic — polyester or nylon.  2. Vegetable — cotton or linen.
3. Animal — wool.  4. Animal — silk.  5. Rayon (regenerated cellulose).

**Part B:**
1. Most synthetics come from petroleum (and historically coal). Rayon and acetate
   are made by regenerating cellulose from wood pulp (semi-synthetic).
2. Synthetic (polyester/polypropylene). The fibers don't absorb water; the
   channels between them pull sweat outward by capillary action to the surface,
   where it spreads out and evaporates fast.
3. Secondary (indirect) transfer.

**Part C:**
1. 2.4 ÷ 8.0 = **0.30**.
2. 7.2 ÷ 8.0 = **0.90** — more soluble in the solvent / less attracted to the
   paper than the #1 dye.
3. **0** (it didn't move).
4. No — the component is carried by the solvent, so it can't move farther than
   the solvent front. If you calculate > 1, you measured from the wrong line or
   past the front.

**Part D:** **Pen B** — it has the same number of spots (3) at the same Rf values
(0.30, 0.55, 0.80). Pen A is missing the middle spot (only 2 spots). Pen C has 3
spots but at different Rf values (0.25/0.50/0.75), so it's a different ink.
