# Homework 13: General Knowledge Sweep I

*Paper track. This one is not tied to a single session — it covers the loose
short-answer items that show up in the "general knowledge / junior detective"
section of real invitational tests. Use the Knowledge Document to check yourself.
No home lab.*

---

## Part A: Powders — uses as identification clues (1 pt each)

Tests often give the **common use** of a powder as a clue. Name the powder (and
formula) from its use:
1. Making plaster / casts
2. Antacid / "stomach aches" (fizzes in water)
3. Chalk; also an antacid and a building material
4. Thickening agent (turns iodine black)
5. "Helps with the common cold" (decolorizes iodine)
6. Flavor enhancer / umami
7. Making concrete
8. Making glass
9. Jello / gelatin desserts
10. Bread (tan pellets, biological)

## Answer Key

**Part A:** 1. Gypsum / plaster of Paris — CaSO₄·2H₂O (½H₂O when calcined).
2. Alka-Seltzer.  3. Calcium carbonate — CaCO₃.  4. Cornstarch — (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ (or
flour).  5. Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) — C₆H₈O₆.  6. MSG — C₅H₈NO₄Na.  7. Sand —
SiO₂ (or gypsum/Portland components).  8. Sand (silica) — SiO₂.  9. Gelatin.
10. Yeast.

**Part B:** 1. Green / blue-green.  2. Bright yellow-orange.  3. Lilac / pale
violet.  4. Orange-red (brick red).  5. Red / crimson.  6. Any of
magnesium/aluminum/zinc/tin/iron give a weak or white-ish flame with no
distinctive color — use density, magnetism (iron), color (copper), and acid
vigor instead. (Copper's green is the one clearly diagnostic flame in the set.)

**Part C:**
1. Any three: cellulose (cotton, wood), starch, cellulose/lignin, natural rubber
   (latex), silk (fibroin), wool/hair (keratin), DNA, proteins.
2. Any three: polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene, PVC, PET, nylon,
   polyester, PMMA, polycarbonate, Bakelite.
3. Regenerated / semi-synthetic — it's made by chemically processing natural
   cellulose from wood pulp, so it starts natural but is manufactured. (Some
   tests just call it "synthetic.")
4. Natural fibers are irregular — uneven thickness, twists, imperfections, scales
   (wool) or ribbon shape (cotton); synthetic fibers are smooth and uniform in
   diameter, often with extrusion lines and delustering pits.

**Part D:**
1. 46 chromosomes; 23 pairs.
2. Ribonucleic acid; single-stranded (vs. double), ribose sugar (vs. deoxyribose),
   uracil replaces thymine.
3. Denaturation (strands separate), annealing (primers bind), extension /
   synthesis (new strand built).
4. DNA polymerase (Taq polymerase).
5. PCR amplifies the tiny DNA sample into millions of copies so there's enough to
   see as visible bands on the gel.
6. Shorter fragments travel farther — they move through the gel's pores more
   easily; DNA's negative charge pulls all fragments toward the positive
   electrode.

**Part E:** Any six of: direction of travel; angle of impact; the velocity /
force involved (low/medium/high); the likely cause (drip, blunt force, gunshot,
cast-off, arterial); the area of convergence and area of origin (where the blood
came from); the position and movement of the victim/attacker; the number of blows
(from cast-off count); whether someone/something was present (a void); blood type;
DNA; sequence/timeline of events; whether the scene was altered.

**Part F:**
1. The stain diameter **increases** as drop height increases, until it reaches a
   terminal size (around 7 feet) and stops growing.
2. Low: large drops > 4 mm, gravity only (dripping wound, pooling); medium:
   1–4 mm, blunt-force/stabbing; high: fine mist < 1 mm, gunshot/explosion.
3. arcsin(5 ÷ 10) = arcsin(0.5) = **30°**.

**Part G:** 1. Bifurcation.  2. Ridge ending.  3. Island / short ridge.
4. Bridge.  5. Lake / enclosure.  6. Delta.

**Part H:**
1. Every contact leaves a trace — material transfers both ways when two objects
   touch.
2. Fingernails and the outer layer of skin (epidermis). (Also accept: hooves,
   horns, feathers, claws.)
3. Androgens.
4. Fingerprints: dactyloscopy. Handwriting: graphology (or questioned-document
   examination).
5. The clumping of red blood cells that occurs when an antibody meets its
   matching antigen — how blood type is read from a test.
6. Any three: paper, thin-layer (TLC), column, gas (GC), high-performance liquid
   (HPLC).
7. Adhesion (water to paper) and cohesion (water to water) — together, capillary
   action.
8. CH₂O.
9. One 8.5" × 11" sheet per participant, both sides, in a sheet protector.
10. Any five: direction of travel; walking vs. running (from stride length and
    toe depth); approximate shoe size; the shoe's tread pattern / type / brand;
    number of people; an injury or unusual gait; approximate weight (from depth
    in a 3-D print); individual defects that identify one specific shoe.
