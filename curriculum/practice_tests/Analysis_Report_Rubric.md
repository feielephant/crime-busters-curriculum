# Analysis Report Rubric

A reusable scoring sheet for the written analysis section, built to mirror how
real invitational keys award points. Use it to score practice tests and to teach
the team what graders look for. The analysis is worth roughly **25%** of the
whole test.

## How real keys score this section

From looking at real answer keys, points come in five buckets:

1. **Naming the correct culprit(s)** — a large fixed block (often ~5–10 points,
   sometimes half if only one of two culprits is named).
2. **Evidence tied to a suspect, with a reason** — ~1 point per item. *"Powder 3
   = gypsum → Suspect A works with drywall."* Evidence with no reason, or a
   reason with no evidence, usually gets partial or nothing.
3. **A plausible motive** for each major suspect — a few points each.
4. **Clearing each non-culprit** — points for explaining why the other suspects
   didn't do it (alibi, evidence is common, motive without evidence).
5. **Timeline / reconstruction** — how the physical evidence fits the sequence of
   events.

**Partial-credit trap:** naming the right culprit with **no supporting evidence**
is often capped at a fraction of the available points. The evidence-with-reasons
is where most of the score actually is.

## The five things graders reward that kids skip

1. **A reason for every piece of evidence.** Not "Powder 3 implicates Suspect A"
   — "Powder 3 is gypsum, and Suspect A does drywall work."
2. **Addressing every suspect, including the innocent ones.** A paragraph per
   non-culprit explaining why they're cleared.
3. **Distinguishing common matches from unusual ones.** "Suspect B also uses
   vinegar and vitamin C, but so do half the suspects — no unique physical
   evidence."
4. **Naming motive-without-evidence and evidence-without-motive explicitly.**
   "Suspect C has the strongest motive — the lawsuit — but zero matching physical
   evidence, so motive alone isn't enough."
5. **A timeline sentence.** How the evidence fits when things happened / whose
   alibi holds.

## Model paragraph (structure to teach)

> **Culprit: Suspect A.** Motive: A was about to be fired and blamed the victim.
>
> Evidence against A:
> - Powder 2 = calcium carbonate (garden lime) — A keeps a bag of lime for the
>   flowerbeds.
> - Fiber 1 = wool (animal) — A wore a wool coat the night of the crime.
> - Soil = sandy loam — matches the lot behind A's shed; the other suspects' home
>   soil is clay or silt loam.
> - Fingerprint = double loop whorl — consistent with A's right index; excludes
>   Suspects C and D, who have no whorls.
>
> Timeline: A has no alibi for the 9–10 p.m. window; B and D were together at a
> meeting until 9:45.
>
> Why the others are cleared:
> - **Suspect B** — the blue cotton fiber and a loop print match, but blue cotton
>   and loop prints are common; no distinctive physical evidence; alibi holds.
> - **Suspect C** — strong motive (was passed over for a promotion) but **no**
>   matching physical evidence, and the whorl fingerprint excludes C. Motive
>   alone isn't proof.
> - **Suspect D** — matches nothing; was with B until 9:45.
