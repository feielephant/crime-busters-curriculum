# Practice-Test Sessions

Three of the ~15 meetings are full practice tests, not teaching. They are where
the team learns to work as a two-person unit under a clock, split the stations,
manage time, and — just as important — clean up before time is called.

| When | Session | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| After Session 6 | **Practice Test 1** | First timed run. Expect it to feel rough. The goal is to learn the *routine*, not to score well. Introduces the answer-review method. |
| After Session 10 | **Practice Test 2** | Mid-season checkpoint. Most topics now taught. Look for which stations are slow and which knowledge gaps are real. |
| Before competition | **Practice Test 3** | Final timed dry run under full competition conditions — goggles, aprons, 50-minute clock, cleanup scored. |

## Where the tests come from

Use real past invitational exams. They are **not** in this repo (copyright), but
they are easy to find — Science Olympiad invitational tests and keys are widely
posted by the hosting schools and on test-exchange sites. Good sources for
Division B Crime Busters: recent invitationals (Oak Valley, Carmel Valley,
Pembroke Hill, and the SSSS summer study-session series). Pick tests your team
has **not** seen. Keep one in reserve for Practice Test 3.

The coach has to assemble the physical samples that go with the written test —
this is real prep. `How_To_Run_A_Practice_Test.md` walks through it: which
samples you can buy or already have from the home-lab kit, which to fake with a
description card ("this plastic floats in water, sinks in isopropyl…"), and how to
set up the room.

## Contents

- `How_To_Run_A_Practice_Test.md` — room setup, sample list, the 50-minute clock,
  what to watch for while they work.
- `Answer_Review_Guide.md` — the structured review that turns a practice test into
  learning: score it together, sort every miss into "didn't know it" vs. "knew it,
  ran out of time" vs. "test-reading error", and feed that back into the next
  weeks.
- `Analysis_Report_Rubric.md` — a reusable scoring sheet for the written analysis
  section, mirroring how real keys award points (each evidence item + a stated
  reason; motive; timeline; every suspect addressed, guilty or not).
