# Session 1: Orientation & Event Overview
### Crime Busters Coach Script — [~90 min]

## Before You Start

- **Learning goals:** By the end, each kid can (1) state the class rules and
  safety expectations in their own words, (2) describe the event format (team
  size, time limit, the four phases of a test, protective gear, cheat sheet
  rules), (3) recite the rough scoring breakdown across the five test sections,
  (4) walk through what a sample test actually looks like end to end, and
  (5) state how the season itself gets graded (homework / quizzes+tests /
  attendance+performance).
- **This is a pure orientation session** — no forensic science content yet.
  That starts next session. This is "how the season runs and how the event is
  scored," plus getting every kid a home practice kit before Session 2's home
  labs start.
- **Materials:**
  - Whiteboard + markers.
  - Printed **home practice kits** — one per kid (see
    `../home_labs/Kit_Assembly_Guide.md`) — plus a copy of the **Home Lab
    Safety Agreement** for each family.
  - Pens/pencils; a sign-in sheet for the parent-volunteer rotation.
  - Optional: a copy of one real past invitational test (kept in `raw/`, not
    part of this repo) just to show the physical format — don't work it today.
- **Prep:**
  - Assemble the home kits ahead of time (about an hour the first time — see
    the Kit Assembly Guide).
  - Write the five test-scoring categories on the whiteboard before kids
    arrive, percentages left blank (they'll guess, like Session 1 used to open
    in year 1).
  - Have the parent-volunteer sign-up sheet ready to pass around.

## Segment-by-Segment Script

### 0:00–0:10 (10 min) — Welcome

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Welcome to Crime Busters! Go around — tell us your name, your grade, and one
  reason you wanted to join."

- **[ACTIVITY: Welcome round]** Every kid answers; coaches introduce
  themselves the same way.

---

### 0:10–0:25 (15 min) — Class Rules & Safety Expectations

- **Goal:** every kid and every parent in the room can state the non-negotiable
  rules before any lab work happens.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Before any science, the rules — because we only get to keep meeting here if
  we follow them.

  **Parent presence is mandatory.** We need at least one parent in the room
  every practice — we'll pass around a sign-up sheet to rotate that. When we
  handle the strong acid (HCl) later in the season, we need one parent **per
  team**, or that team doesn't get to practice with it.

  **Safety rules, all the time:** goggles on for any hands-on work, follow
  instructions exactly, no tasting anything (even the food-grade stuff in your
  home kit), smell by wafting the air toward your nose — never straight from
  the container. The strong acid is handled here, with a coach, never at home.

  **Respect this room.** No mess left behind, nothing broken, nothing
  inappropriate — or we lose access to the room and practice stops. That
  applies to before, during, *and* after class, including while waiting for
  pickup.

  **Other basics:** be on time (including pickup), be respectful to coaches and
  teammates, no phones/games/toys, no running, no yelling, no food or drinks on
  site, stay focused — no side conversations during teaching time.

  One more thing, and it's serious: **repeated misbehavior means being removed
  from the team.** We say this once, clearly, on day one, so nobody's surprised
  later."

- **[ASK THE TEAM: Why do you think "no mess left behind" is such a big deal for
  a team like ours, specifically?]** — Listen for: we're guests in this space;
  losing the room means the whole team loses practice time, not just one kid.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** Ask two or three kids to each state one rule back in their
  own words, until the whole list has been said out loud by someone.

---

### 0:25–0:45 (20 min) — Quick Overview of the Event

- **Goal:** kids can describe the event's shape well enough to know what
  they're training for.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Here's the shape of the actual event. Crime Busters is a **Division B**
  event — there's a related event called Forensics in Division C, but that's
  not us. You compete in **teams of two**. You wear protective gear — goggles,
  gloves, lab coats or old clothes. You get **50 minutes**, and in that time
  you do four things: **lab work, answering theory questions, writing your
  final analysis, and cleanup** — and time management across all four is
  genuinely the hardest part of this event, harder than any single science
  fact. You're each allowed **one cheat sheet** (two per team), 8.5 by 11
  inches, one side or both — we'll get into the rules on that later.

  The evidence types you'll see across a season: powders (sometimes mixed 2–3
  together), liquids, metals, plastics/polymers, hair/fibers, fingerprints,
  blood, DNA, soil/tire tracks/shoe prints, and chromatography. We're going to
  cover every one of these this season."

- **[ASK THE TEAM: Of everything on that evidence list, which one are you most
  curious about, and which one sounds hardest?]** — No wrong answers; just
  gets them oriented to the season ahead.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** Ask: "How many minutes do you get, and what are the four
  things you have to fit inside that time?" (50 minutes; lab + theory +
  analysis + cleanup.)

---

### 0:45–1:05 (20 min) — How the Test Is Actually Scored

- **Goal:** kids internalize where the points really are, so they know where
  practice time should go all season.

- **[ACTIVITY: Guess the Percentages]**
  On the whiteboard you have the five categories from Prep. Have the team
  guess what percent of the score each is worth (they should total 100). Let
  them argue it out, then reveal the real numbers:

  | Category | Worth |
  |---|---|
  | Qualitative analysis (powders, liquids, metals) | **~50%** |
  | Analysis writeup (identify the culprit, justify it) | **~25%** |
  | Physical evidence (fingerprints, DNA, tracks, soil, spatter) | ~10% |
  | Polymers (hair, fibers, plastics) | ~10% |
  | Paper chromatography | ~5% |

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Look at the top two: qualitative analysis plus the analysis writeup are
  **75% of the whole test.** That's exactly why this curriculum spends the
  most time there. The 'cool' physical evidence — DNA, blood spatter,
  fingerprints — is only about a quarter of the score combined.

  A few more scoring things to know: cleanup is graded too — **losing 10% for
  not cleaning up as instructed** is a real penalty, so budget time for it,
  don't treat it as optional at the very end. Bringing prohibited items —
  an oversized or extra cheat sheet, a programmable or graphing calculator —
  costs another **10%**. And if scores tie, the tiebreakers are: correctness
  of the final analysis, then how many unknowns you got right in qualitative
  analysis, then how many you got right in polymers/plastics."

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "If you had 50 minutes and had to protect time for one
  section, which is it?" (Qualitative analysis — half the points, and slow.)
  "Name one penalty and how big it is." (10% for not cleaning up as
  instructed, or 10% for prohibited items.)

---

### 1:05–1:20 (15 min) — Walking Through a Sample Test

- **Goal:** kids can describe the three-part shape of an actual test paper
  before they ever see one for real.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Every test has the same three-part shape.

  **Part one: the Crime Scene page.** A detailed description of the scene.
  Read it closely — little details here matter later, when you're deciding who
  did it.

  **Part two: Evidence Testing & Qualitative Analysis.** You run labs, answer
  knowledge questions about what you find, and start connecting each piece of
  evidence back to a specific suspect from the crime scene page.

  **Part three: Final Analysis.** You pull everything together and name the
  culprit — with written reasoning. Be thorough here; this is worth a full
  quarter of your score, and it's also a tiebreaker."

- **[ASK THE TEAM: Why do you think reading the crime scene page carefully,
  before doing any lab work, actually saves you time later?]** — Listen for:
  you'll know what to look for and which suspects to compare evidence against,
  instead of re-reading it later under time pressure.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** Ask the team to say the three parts back, in order, from
  memory.

---

### 1:20–1:26 (6 min) — How the Season Itself Is Graded

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Quick note on how *we* grade the season, separate from the actual
  competition: **homework is 40%, quizzes and tests are 40%, and
  attendance/classroom performance is 20%.** Homework matters as much as
  quizzes — showing up prepared and doing the take-home work is not optional
  extra credit, it's a real chunk of how you're doing."

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "What three things make up your season grade, and roughly
  how much is each worth?" (Homework 40%, quizzes/tests 40%, attendance and
  performance 20%.)

---

### 1:26–1:30 (4 min) — Home Practice Kits + Safety Agreement

- **Goal:** every kid leaves with a kit, every family has the safety
  agreement, and both are ready before Session 2's home labs start.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Last thing today, and it's the fun part. Qualitative analysis is half the
  test, and the only way to get fast at it is reps — so here's a kit for each
  of you to keep at home.

  Inside: little labeled bags of food-grade powders from a kitchen — baking
  soda, cornstarch, salt, sugar, Epsom salt, cream of tartar, citric acid,
  chalk, sand. A dropper bottle of iodine — the same weak stuff sold at the
  pharmacy for cuts. A bottle of purple cabbage juice, a pH indicator (it's
  literally boiled cabbage water). From home you'll add: white vinegar, water,
  a magnet, some small cups, toothpicks.

  Here are the hard rules — your parent signs a sheet saying they understand
  them: an adult is in the room every time, always; goggles or glasses on;
  never taste anything, ever, even though it's food — that habit matters at
  competition, where the samples are *not* food; smell by wafting, never stick
  your nose in; no strong chemicals, no heat, no flames at home — the strong
  acid, the HCl, only ever happens here, with a coach; and clean up
  completely every time, since cleanup is scored at competition and we
  practice that habit starting now."

- **[ACTIVITY:]** Hand each kid their kit. Have them open it, check contents
  against the safety sheet's list, and read the rules out loud, taking turns.
  Send the **Home Lab Safety Agreement** home — it must come back signed
  before their first home lab (assigned after Session 2).

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "What are the three things that never happen at home?"
  (Strong chemicals, heat, flames.) "What always happens?" (An adult in the
  room; goggles on; cleanup after.)

---

## Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Recap of today, since none of it is science yet but all of it matters all
  season:
  1. The rules exist so we keep the room and keep meeting — parent presence,
     safety, respect for the space.
  2. Teams of 2, 50 minutes, four phases: lab, theory, analysis, cleanup.
  3. Scoring is **75% qualitative analysis + analysis writeup** — that's where
     practice time goes.
  4. Every test has the same shape: crime scene page → testing → final
     analysis.
  5. Your season grade: homework 40%, quizzes/tests 40%, attendance/
     performance 20%.
  6. You've got your kit and the safety agreement — get it signed before next
     time."

- **Exit ticket** (say it out loud or on a slip; answers for you):
  1. **Name two of the four things you have to fit into 50 minutes at
     competition.** *(Any two of: lab work, theory questions, analysis
     writeup, cleanup.)*
  2. **What two categories together make up about 75% of the test score?**
     *(Qualitative analysis (~50%) and the analysis writeup (~25%).)*
  3. **What's one penalty you could get during a test, and roughly how big is
     it?** *(10% for not cleaning up as instructed, or 10% for a prohibited
     item like an extra/oversized cheat sheet or a non-Class-II calculator.)*

- **Homework:** none yet — the first academic homework starts with Session 2.
  Just get the Home Lab Safety Agreement signed and returned.

## Coach Notes & Common Kid Mistakes

- **Kids (and parents) treat orientation as skippable.** It isn't — the
  parent-presence rule and the HCl-per-team rule specifically are easy to
  forget by the time they matter (weeks into the season). Repeat them at the
  start of the session where HCl first comes up.
- **"75% qualitative analysis" doesn't sink in until it's repeated.** Kids
  will still want to spend disproportionate practice time on the more
  exciting evidence types (blood spatter, fingerprints). Come back to this
  percentage breakdown periodically across the season.
- **Cleanup gets treated as an afterthought.** Since it's graded, frame it
  from day one as a timed part of the test, not something you do "if there's
  time left."
- **Forgetting the kit rules by Session 2.** Re-ask the "never at home /
  always at home" questions at the start of Session 2 before any home lab
  gets assigned.
