# Session 6: Metals
### Crime Busters Coach Script — [~90 min]

## Before You Start

- **Learning goals:** Each kid can (1) identify the six recommended metals (Mg,
  Al, Fe, Zn, Sn, Cu) by color, magnetism, density, and reaction with acid,
  (2) calculate density from mass and volume and convert between g/cm³ and kg/m³,
  (3) name the four basic reaction types and classify a metal-in-acid reaction as
  single replacement, (4) explain the oxide layers on aluminum and magnesium and
  why aluminum resists acid at first, and (5) name the metals in steel, brass,
  and bronze.
- **Year 1 recap built in:** last year's metals homework — the list and the
  reactivity ranking.
- **Materials:**
  - Knowledge Document Chapter 6. Read first.
  - **Known metal samples**, labeled: magnesium (ribbon or turnings — handle
    carefully, do NOT ignite), aluminum (foil ball or bar), iron (nail or
    filings), zinc (galvanized washer or granules), tin (foil or a pewter-free
    piece), copper (wire or pipe). If you can't get all six, prioritize Al, Fe,
    Cu, Zn.
  - **Unknown metal set** (numbered 1–4): a subset of the above.
  - Per pair: a strong magnet, a small digital scale (0.1 g), a 10 mL graduated
    cylinder, water, hand lens, paper towels.
  - 1 M HCl + goggles + gloves + tray — **coach runs the acid tests** at a front
    station this session (small samples, well ventilated).
  - Calculators; printed mini periodic table; printed **Density Problems sheet**,
    **Metal ID sheet**, **Reaction-Type Sort cards** (below). Whiteboard.
- **Prep:** label knowns; number unknowns and write your key; pre-cut identical
  small pieces of 2–3 metals for the acid demo; print sheets. Test the water
  displacement method yourself with one metal.
- **Vocabulary:** density · displacement · activity series · passivation · oxide
  · alloy · synthesis / decomposition / single replacement / double replacement ·
  atomic number · ferromagnetic.

> **If you only have 60 minutes:** Cut the reaction-type sort (1:05–1:15) — teach
> the four types in 3 minutes and move the sorting to Homework 6. Do the metal ID
> with 3 unknowns instead of 4.

## Segment-by-Segment Script

### 0:00–0:07 (7 min) — Recap & Hook

- **[ASK THE TEAM:]** "From last year's homework — rank aluminum, zinc, tin, and
  magnesium from most reactive with acid to least." (Mg > Al > Zn > Sn — roughly)
  "Which metal on our list is magnetic?" (iron)

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Last year you learned the list and the reactivity order. This year we add the
  parts the harder tests want: **density calculations** — with unit conversions
  that trip everybody up — **atomic numbers**, the **reaction types**, and the
  **oxide layers** that explain why aluminum acts weird with acid. And you'll do
  a real metal ID with displacement measurements."

---

### 0:07–0:27 (20 min) — Direct Teaching: The Six Metals

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Five ways to tell them apart, in the order you should use them:"

  Build the table:

  | Metal | Symbol | Atomic # | Density (g/cm³) | Color | Magnet? | With HCl |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Magnesium | Mg | 12 | 1.74 | dull silver | no | vigorous, fast |
  | Aluminum | Al | 13 | 2.70 | shiny silver | no | slow start, then steady |
  | Iron | Fe | 26 | 7.87 | gray (may rust) | **YES** | slow–moderate; solution goes pale green |
  | Zinc | Zn | 30 | 7.14 | bluish-gray | no | moderate, steady |
  | Tin | Sn | 50 | 7.27 | soft silver | no | very slow / barely |
  | Copper | Cu | 29 | 8.96 | **reddish-orange** | no | **nothing** |

  "**1. Color** — copper is the giveaway, reddish. Everything else is gray/silver.
  **2. Magnet** — only iron sticks. (Nickel and cobalt do too, but they're
  usually not in the set.)
  **3. Density** — Mg and Al are *much* lighter than the rest. If it feels
  surprisingly light for its size, it's one of those two.
  **4. Acid** — the vigor ranks Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Sn > Cu-does-nothing. This is
  a shortened **activity series**.
  **5. Feel** — tin and aluminum are soft; iron is hard; magnesium ribbon bends
  easily.
  **6. Flame test** — the supervisor may give you a candle, or just tell you the
  flame color. Of our six metals, only **copper burns green**. The rest give a
  weak white-ish flame with no useful color, so you fall back on density, the
  magnet, and acid. Watch out — a speck of salt turns *any* flame bright yellow,
  and that's sodium, not your sample."

- **[ACTIVITY: Sort the Knowns]**
  Pass around the labeled known metals. Each kid: hold each one, try the magnet,
  note color and how heavy it feels. Fill in the "color / magnet / heavy-light"
  columns of a table from direct observation.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Blindfold test: I hand you a gray metal, it's not magnetic,
  and it feels really light. Two candidates?" (magnesium or aluminum — then
  density or acid separates them)

---

### 0:27–0:45 (18 min) — Direct Teaching: Density Calculations

- **Goal:** kids can compute density and, critically, handle the units.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "**Density = mass ÷ volume.** Rearranged: mass = density × volume; volume =
  mass ÷ density.

  To get the volume of an odd-shaped chunk of metal, use **water displacement**:
  fill the graduated cylinder to a known level, drop the metal in, read the new
  level, subtract. The difference is the metal's volume in milliliters, and
  **1 mL = 1 cm³**.

  Then mass ÷ volume gives density in **grams per cm³**. Compare to the table.

  Now the trap: some test questions give density in **kilograms per cubic meter**
  instead. The conversion: **1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.** So aluminum is 2.70 g/cm³ =
  2700 kg/m³.

  And watch cube math. If a question says 'a 1.5-meter cube of aluminum':
  - volume = 1.5 m × 1.5 m × 1.5 m = (1.5)³ = **3.375 m³**
  - mass = 2700 kg/m³ × 3.375 m³ = **9112.5 kg**

  If instead it says 'a volume of 1.5 cubic meters,' then volume is just 1.5 m³
  and mass = 2700 × 1.5 = 4050 kg. **Read the wording.**"

- **[ACTIVITY: Measure a Known Metal's Density]**
  Each pair weighs a known metal sample, measures its volume by displacement,
  computes density, and checks it against the table. Do it for two metals.

- **[ACTIVITY: Density Problems sheet]** — kids work the word problems (below),
  including one kg/m³ conversion and one cube.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Density of water in g/mL?" (1.00 — memorize it) "Convert
  7.87 g/cm³ to kg/m³." (7870)

---

### 0:45–1:00 (15 min) — Main Activity: Identify the Unknown Metals

- **[ACTIVITY: Metal ID]**
  Each pair gets numbered unknowns 1–4 and the Metal ID sheet. They do: color,
  magnet, mass, volume by displacement → density. They fill everything except the
  acid column.

  Then bring their samples (or matched pieces) to the **front acid station**,
  where the **coach** adds a drop or two of 1 M HCl to a small piece of each and
  the pair records the vigor. Kids stay in goggles.

  They name each metal with symbol and atomic number.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** For each pair, point at one ID and ask "which two
  measurements were decisive?" (usually density + magnet, or density + acid
  vigor)

---

### 1:00–1:15 (15 min) — Direct Teaching: Reaction Types, Oxides, Alloys

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "**Four reaction types** — this is multiple choice on tests:
  - **Synthesis** (or combination): two things join. A + B → AB.
    `2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO`.
  - **Decomposition**: one thing splits. AB → A + B. `2 H₂O → 2 H₂ + O₂`.
  - **Single replacement**: an element kicks out another from a compound.
    A + BC → AC + B. **A metal dissolving in acid is this one:**
    `Zn + 2 HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂` — zinc replaces the hydrogen.
  - **Double replacement**: two compounds swap partners. AB + CD → AD + CB.
    `AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃`.

  **Oxide layers** — why aluminum is weird:
  - Aluminum instantly grows a thin, tough, clear layer of **aluminum oxide,
    Al₂O₃**, the moment it touches air. That layer *protects* the metal — it's
    called **passivation** — and it's why aluminum doesn't corrode away like iron
    and why it's slow to start reacting with acid (the acid has to eat through the
    oxide first).
  - Magnesium grows a dull white **magnesium oxide, MgO**, coat in air. The
    reaction `2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO` is a **synthesis** reaction — a common question.
  - Iron's oxide — **rust, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O** — is flaky and does *not* protect the
    metal, so iron keeps rusting all the way through.

  **Alloys** — a metal mixed with other elements for better properties:
  - **Steel = iron + carbon.**
  - **Brass = copper + zinc.**
  - **Bronze = copper + tin.**"

- **[ACTIVITY: Reaction-Type Sort]**
  Deal out the Reaction-Type Sort cards (equations). In pairs, kids sort each into
  synthesis / decomposition / single replacement / double replacement.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Magnesium burning in air to make magnesium oxide — which
  type?" (synthesis) "Iron plus copper sulfate makes iron sulfate plus copper —
  which type?" (single replacement) "Steel is an alloy of what?" (iron)

### 1:15–1:27 (12 min) — Consolidation + Practice-Test Preview

- **[ACTIVITY:]** Kids build the **metals block** of their note sheet: the table
  (symbol, atomic number, density, color, magnetic, acid vigor), the density
  formula with the unit conversion, the four reaction types, and the three
  alloys.

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Next meeting is **Practice Test 1** — a full timed test, everything we've done
  so far plus whatever else is on it. It's going to feel rough and that's the
  point — we're learning the *routine*: how to split the stations between the two
  of you, how to watch the clock, how to clean up before time is called. Bring
  your note sheets. Wear clothes you can cover with an apron."

### 1:27–1:30 (3 min) — Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket

- **Exit ticket:**
  1. **A non-magnetic gray metal has density 2.70 g/cm³. What is it, and what's
     its atomic number?** *(Aluminum; 13)*
  2. **Why does aluminum resist acid at first?** *(A protective aluminum-oxide
     (Al₂O₃) passivation layer forms in air; the acid must dissolve it before
     reaching the metal.)*
  3. **`Zn + 2 HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂` — what reaction type?** *(Single replacement)*

- **Homework:** Homework 6 — paper track (density and unit-conversion problems +
  reaction-type classification). **No home lab** — the metals kit doesn't go
  home; the hands-on ID happens at practice.

## Density Problems Sheet (print for kids)

Show your work and units.

1. A metal cube 2.0 cm on each side has a mass of 63.0 g. What is its density?
   Which metal is it?
2. A chunk of metal raises the water in a graduated cylinder from 20.0 mL to
   28.0 mL and weighs 71.7 g. Density? Metal?
3. Convert 8.96 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
4. A solid aluminum bar has a volume of 40 cm³. What is its mass? (Al = 2.70
   g/cm³)
5. A 0.5-meter cube of iron — what is its mass in kg? (Fe = 7870 kg/m³)

*(Answers: 1) 63.0 ÷ 8.0 = 7.88 g/cm³ → iron.  2) vol 8.0 mL → 71.7 ÷ 8.0 = 8.96
g/cm³ → copper.  3) 8960 kg/m³.  4) 2.70 × 40 = 108 g.  5) (0.5)³ = 0.125 m³ ×
7870 = 983.75 kg.)*

- `2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O`  *(synthesis)*
- `CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂`  *(decomposition)*
- `Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu`  *(single replacement)*
- `AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃`  *(double replacement)*
- `2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO`  *(synthesis)*
- `Zn + 2 HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂`  *(single replacement)*
- `2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂`  *(decomposition)*
- `HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O`  *(double replacement / neutralization)*

## Coach Notes & Common Kid Mistakes

- **Igniting magnesium.** Do not let anyone hold magnesium ribbon near a flame —
  it burns with a blinding white light and eye damage is real. Keep it away from
  the acid demo's heat too. Handle, observe, done.
- **Unit-conversion errors.** g/cm³ ↔ kg/m³ is ×1000, and cube volume is s³ not
  s². These are the two things graded questions punish. Drill both.
- **Reading the water level wrong.** Read at the bottom of the meniscus, eye
  level. A parallax error throws the density off enough to name the wrong metal.
- **Forgetting copper does nothing with HCl.** "No reaction" is itself the
  identification — copper is the only listed metal that doesn't react with
  hydrochloric acid.
- **Calling the metal-in-acid reaction "synthesis."** It's **single replacement**
  — the metal replaces hydrogen, and hydrogen gas bubbles off.
- **Mixing up which oxide protects.** Al₂O₃ and MgO form protective-ish layers;
  rust (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O) does not. That's why we still have ancient bronze artifacts
  but iron ones rust to nothing.
