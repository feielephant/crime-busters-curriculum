# Session 7: Hair & DNA Evidence
### Crime Busters Coach Script — [~90 min]

## Before You Start

- **Learning goals:** Each kid can (1) name the hair layers inner-to-outer and the
  three cuticle scale patterns, (2) list the four growth phases in order with
  rough durations and which phase most hairs are in, (3) calculate a medullary
  index and use the human (<1/3) vs. animal (>1/2) cutoffs, (4) explain what an
  anagen root vs. a shed telogen club root means for DNA, (5) explain the
  "hair keeps growing after death" myth and the arsenic-timeline use, and
  (6) read a DNA gel to match a crime-scene sample to a suspect.
- **Year 1 recap built in:** hair structure, scale patterns, human/dog/cat basics,
  DNA structure and gel electrophoresis (year 1 covered DNA well).
- **Materials:**
  - Knowledge Document Chapters 8 and 12. Read first.
  - **Microscope** if you have one (the supervisor may provide one at
    competition), plus prepared or homemade slides: a few human hairs (different
    people), dog hair, cat hair. Clear tape + index cards work for quick mounts.
  - Printed **hair micrographs** with a scale bar (find royalty-free forensic hair
    images, or use the ones in the topic decks for your own reference — do not
    put copyrighted images in the repo) and rulers, so kids can measure medullary
    index even without a scope.
  - Printed **Growth-Phase Cards**, **Medullary Index sheet**, **Gel-Matching
    Worksheet** (below). Whiteboard.
- **Prep:** make hair slides; print the micrograph packet and worksheets; work
  the gel worksheet yourself.
- **Vocabulary:** cuticle · cortex · medulla · coronal / spinous / imbricate ·
  medullary index · anagen / catagen / telogen / exogen · follicle · keratin ·
  melanin · club root · gel electrophoresis · nucleotide · STR · mitochondrial
  vs. nuclear DNA.

> **If you only have 60 minutes:** Cut the microscope slide-making (do coach demo
> slides only) and shorten the medullary-index activity to the printed
> micrographs. Keep the growth cycle and the gel matching — those are the year-2
> additions.

## Segment-by-Segment Script

### 0:00–0:07 (7 min) — Recap & Hook

- **[ASK THE TEAM:]** "Three layers of a hair, inside to outside?" (medulla,
  cortex, cuticle) "What are the cuticle scale patterns called?" (coronal,
  spinous, imbricate) "What's hair made of?" (keratin)

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "You know the parts of a hair. This year the tests want more: the **growth
  cycle** with actual timeframes, the medullary index as a **calculation** with
  a number cutoff, why a *pulled* hair is better for DNA than a *shed* one, and a
  couple of classic 'gotcha' questions — does hair grow after death, what
  poisoning does hair reveal. Plus we'll practice reading a DNA gel for a match."

---

### 0:07–0:22 (15 min) — Direct Teaching: The Growth Cycle & the Medullary Index

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "**Four growth phases**, in order — memorize them:

  | Phase | What happens | How long | Share of your scalp hairs |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Anagen** | active growth | 2–7 years | ~85–90% |
  | **Catagen** | transition — follicle shrinks, hair cut off from blood supply | ~2–3 weeks | ~1% |
  | **Telogen** | resting — anchored but not growing | ~2–4 months | ~10–15% |
  | **Exogen** | shedding — old hair falls, new anagen hair pushes up | overlaps telogen | — |

  Why it matters:
  - A hair **pulled out during anagen** brings a soft root with tissue and lots
    of **nuclear DNA** — the best kind.
  - A hair that **shed naturally in telogen** has a hard, dry, **club-shaped
    root** and little or no nuclear DNA — only **mitochondrial DNA**.
  - Human scalp hair grows about **1 cm a month** — roughly **half an inch a
    month, six inches a year**. Growth is a bit faster in summer; the *shedding*
    peak is autumn.

  **Medullary index** — now a calculation:
  **MI = medulla diameter ÷ whole hair-shaft diameter.**
  - **Human: MI less than 1/3** (about 0.33) — thin or missing medulla.
  - **Animal: MI more than 1/2** (about 0.5) — the medulla fills most of the
    shaft.
  Example: medulla 12 µm, shaft 48 µm → 12/48 = **0.25 → human.**"

- **[ACTIVITY: Growth-Phase Card Sort]**
  Deal the Growth-Phase Cards (each has a description or a duration or a "share of
  hairs"). Kids arrange them into the four phases in order.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Which phase are most of your hairs in right now?" (anagen)
  "A hair at the scene has a club root and no tissue — pulled or shed? Good DNA?"
  (shed, telogen; poor nuclear DNA, mito only)

---

### 0:22–0:40 (18 min) — Activity: Measure Medullary Index & Compare Species

- **[ACTIVITY: Under the Scope / Micrographs]**
  - If you have a microscope: pairs mount a hair (tape it to an index card with a
    window cut, or use a slide), view it, and sketch the medulla and cuticle
    scales. Rotate human, dog, cat.
  - Everyone: on the printed **micrograph packet**, measure the medulla width and
    the shaft width with a ruler (the images have a scale bar), compute the
    medullary index, and decide human vs. animal. Do 4–5 hairs.
  - Fill the **Medullary Index sheet**: for each hair — medulla width, shaft
    width, MI, human/animal, and the cuticle pattern if visible.

  Species cues to reinforce:
  - **Human**: imbricate (flat) scales; medulla thin/fragmented/absent; even
    pigment; club root.
  - **Dog**: imbricate or coronal scales; medulla continuous and broad (MI ≥ 1/2);
    may be banded color; spade-shaped root.
  - **Cat**: spinous (petal) scales; medulla continuous, often a "string of
    pearls"; very large relative to shaft; frayed root.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "You measure medulla 20 µm, shaft 30 µm. MI? Human or animal?"
  (0.67 → animal) "Petal-shaped cuticle scales — which species?" (cat)

---

### 0:40–0:52 (12 min) — Direct Teaching: The Classic Hair Questions

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Three questions that come up every year:

  **'Does hair keep growing after death?'** No. After death the skin
  **dehydrates and pulls back** from the base of the hairs and nails, making them
  *look* longer. No new growth.

  **'The absorbent property of hair makes it important in what kind of poisoning,
  and why?'** **Arsenic** (and other heavy metals). Arsenic **binds to the
  keratin** in hair and stays locked in place as the hair grows. So the hair
  keeps a **timeline** — cut it into segments along its length and you can tell
  *when* the exposure happened and whether it was one dose or chronic.

  **'Why is a forcibly removed hair more useful than a shed one?'** The pulled
  hair carries the **follicle and root sheath tissue**, which is full of nuclear
  DNA. A shed hair is just the dead shaft — mitochondrial DNA only, which is
  shared by everyone in a maternal line, so it's much less specific."

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Hair 'grows' on a corpse — true or false, and why?" (False —
  skin retracts.) "What metal does hair reveal, and how?" (arsenic — binds
  keratin, preserves a datable timeline)

---

### 0:52–1:00 (8 min) — Direct Teaching: Reading a DNA Gel

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "You covered DNA structure last year. The Crime Busters task is usually:
  **here's a gel, which suspect matches the crime scene?**

  Gel electrophoresis sorts DNA pieces by **size**. DNA has a **negative**
  charge, so it moves toward the **positive** end of the gel. **Smaller pieces
  move farther**; big pieces stay near the wells. You get a pattern of bands in
  each lane — like a barcode.

  To find the match: put the **crime-scene lane** next to each **suspect lane**
  and check whether the bands line up at the **same heights**. The suspect whose
  barcode matches the crime scene is your source. There may be a 'ladder' lane of
  known sizes for scale — ignore it for matching.

  The DNA itself comes from blood, saliva, skin/touch cells, semen, or **hair
  with the root attached**."

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Which way do the small fragments travel, and why?" (farther,
  toward the positive electrode, because DNA is negatively charged and small
  pieces move through the gel faster)

---

### 1:00–1:18 (18 min) — Main Activity: Gel-Matching Worksheet + Combined Case

- **[ACTIVITY: Gel Matching]**
  Hand out the Gel-Matching Worksheet — a crime-scene lane and 4 suspect lanes
  drawn as band patterns. Kids identify the matching suspect and write a sentence
  of reasoning ("Suspect 3's bands align with the crime-scene lane at all four
  positions").

  Then a **mini integration**: a short scenario with a hair found at the scene.
  Kids state (a) the species from a described micrograph, (b) whether the root
  suggests it was pulled or shed and what that means for DNA, (c) which suspect
  the gel points to, and (d) a one-line analysis tying it together.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "The gel points to Suspect 2 but the hair is dog hair and
  Suspect 2 has no dog. What do you conclude?" (the gel is from a *human* sample —
  probably a different piece of evidence; the dog hair points elsewhere; don't
  force one suspect to explain everything)

---

### 1:18–1:26 (8 min) — Consolidation

- **[ACTIVITY:]** Kids build the **hair block** of their note sheet: layers,
  scale patterns, the 4 growth phases with durations, the MI formula and cutoffs,
  the club-root/DNA point, and the two gotcha answers (postmortem, arsenic). Plus
  a **DNA note**: gel = size separation, negative charge → positive end, small =
  farther, match the barcode.

---

### 1:26–1:30 (4 min) — Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket

- **Exit ticket:**
  1. **List the four hair growth phases in order.** *(Anagen, catagen, telogen,
     exogen)*
  2. **Medulla 15 µm, shaft 60 µm. Medullary index? Human or animal?**
     *(0.25 → human)*
  3. **On a DNA gel, which fragments travel farthest and why?** *(The smallest —
     DNA is negatively charged and moves toward the positive electrode; smaller
     pieces move through the gel faster)*

- **Homework:** Homework 7 — paper track (medullary index calcs + gel band
  matching) + **Home Lab 7** (mount and examine your own hair vs. a pet's — only
  if a magnifier or scope is available at home; otherwise paper-only).

## Coach Notes & Common Kid Mistakes

- **Reversing the medullary-index cutoffs.** Human is the *small* one (<1/3);
  animal is *large* (>1/2). Mnemonic: "humans have hardly any medulla."
- **Mixing up cuticle patterns.** Coronal = crown/stack (tiny animals);
  **spinous = cat** (petals/spines); **imbricate = human and dog** (flat
  shingles).
- **Thinking a shed hair is worthless.** It still gives species ID, color,
  treatment, and mitochondrial DNA — just not a full nuclear profile.
- **Believing the postmortem-growth myth.** It's the skin retracting. Say it
  three times.
- **On the gel: matching band *count* instead of band *position*.** Two lanes can
  both have 4 bands but at different heights — that's not a match. Line them up.
- **Forcing one suspect to explain every piece of evidence.** A dog hair and a
  human DNA gel can point to two different people. Let the evidence say what it
  says.
