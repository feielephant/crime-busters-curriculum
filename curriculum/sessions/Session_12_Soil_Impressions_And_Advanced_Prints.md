# Session 12: Soil, Impressions & Advanced Prints
### Crime Busters Coach Script — [~90 min]

## Before You Start

- **Learning goals:** Each kid can (1) use the soil texture triangle to turn
  %sand/silt/clay into a texture-class name, (2) state the particle-size cutoffs
  and what soil color and pH indicate, (3) classify a fingerprint into the full
  IAFIS eight-pattern set, (4) define latent / patent / plastic prints and name
  how latents are developed, (5) define dermatoglyphics, dactyloscopy,
  adermatoglyphia, and hexadactyly and say which skin layer prints form in, and
  (6) separate class from individual characteristics on a shoeprint.
- **Year 1 recap built in:** soil drainage by type; the three basic fingerprint
  patterns and minutiae; basic shoe/tire direction.
- **Materials:**
  - Knowledge Document Chapters 16 and 17. Read first.
  - Printed **soil texture triangles** (USDA — freely available from USDA/NRCS),
    one per kid, plus the **Soil Triangle sheet** (below).
  - **Soil jar test** setup: a clear jar with lid, a scoop of garden soil, water,
    a little dish soap — start this at the top of class so it settles by the end.
  - Fingerprint station: washable ink pad or pencil-graphite + tape, white cards,
    hand lenses; printed **IAFIS 8-pattern reference** (public-domain FBI
    pattern charts).
  - Printed **shoeprint comparison pair** (one "crime scene" print, one "suspect"
    print, with a matching individual defect), the **Print Classification sheet**
    and **Shoeprint sheet** (below).
  - Whiteboard.
- **Prep:** print the triangles and pattern charts; set up the settling jar;
  ink-pad ready; print all sheets; practice reading the triangle yourself with
  three examples.
- **Vocabulary:** soil texture triangle · sand / silt / clay (with sizes) ·
  loam · gley · IAFIS · plain / tented arch · radial / ulnar loop · plain /
  central-pocket / double-loop / accidental whorl · latent / patent / plastic
  print · ninhydrin · cyanoacrylate fuming · dermatoglyphics · dactyloscopy ·
  adermatoglyphia · hexadactyly · class vs. individual characteristics.

> **If you only have 60 minutes:** Cut the shoeprint comparison (move to
> homework). Do the soil triangle and the fingerprint classification station —
> those carry the most points.

### 0:06–0:26 (20 min) — Direct Teaching + Practice: The Soil Texture Triangle

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Soil is a mix of three particle sizes:

  | Particle | Size |
  |---|---|
  | **Sand** | 2.0 – 0.05 mm — gritty, you can see grains |
  | **Silt** | 0.05 – 0.002 mm — smooth, floury |
  | **Clay** | **less than 0.002 mm** — sticky wet, hard dry |

  (Anything bigger than 2 mm is gravel and doesn't count.)

  The **texture triangle** takes the **percent sand, silt, and clay** — they add
  to 100 — and gives a **class name**. Here's how to read it:
  - **% clay**: find it on the **left** side, follow that line **straight across**
    (horizontal).
  - **% sand**: find it on the **bottom**, follow the line **up and to the left**.
  - **% silt**: find it on the **right** side, follow the line **down and to the
    left**.
  - Where your three lines meet is the class.

  Anchors to memorize:
  - **Loam** ≈ 40% sand / 40% silt / 20% clay — the balanced center.
  - **Sandy loam** — mostly sand (~60–70%), low clay.
  - **Silt loam** — mostly silt (>50%), low clay.
  - **Clay** — more than 40% clay.
  - **Clay loam** — ~30% clay, roughly even sand and silt.

  Examples:
  - 60% sand, 30% silt, 10% clay → **sandy loam**
  - 35% clay, 40% silt, 25% sand → **clay loam**
  - 20% sand, 60% silt, 20% clay → **silt loam**"

- **[ACTIVITY: Soil Triangle sheet]**
  Kids plot 5 given compositions on their own triangle and name each class. Then
  the reverse: "a soil is called *sandy clay* — roughly what's the clay range?"
  (about 35–55% clay).

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "45% sand, 40% silt, 15% clay — what class?" (loam) "Particle
  size of clay?" (< 0.002 mm)

### 0:26–0:36 (10 min) — Direct Teaching: Soil Color, pH & Forensic Value

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "**Color** tells you about the soil's history:

  | Color | Means |
  |---|---|
  | red / yellow / orange | **iron oxides** — well-drained, oxygen-rich |
  | dark brown / black | **organic matter** (humus) — fertile topsoil |
  | gray / blue-gray | **waterlogged**, oxygen-poor (iron chemically reduced) |
  | white / pale / cream | **carbonates, salts, or lime** — or heavily leached |

  **pH** runs about 4 (acidic — pine forest) to 9 (alkaline — arid, limey). A
  garden that's had lime or ammonia added reads differently from the dirt around
  it — a useful comparison point.

  **Forensic value:** soil is **class evidence**. You compare soil on a suspect's
  shoes or tires to soil at the scene — texture, color, pH, mineral grains,
  little bits of plant matter — to **place them there or rule them out**. It
  rarely proves one exact location, but a distinctive soil (bright red clay, a
  specific gravel) can be a strong link."

- **[ACTIVITY:]** Check the settling jar — sand at the bottom, then silt, then
  clay (still cloudy on top). Kids estimate the rough percentages from the layer
  thicknesses and name the class.

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Soil at the scene is bright red. What does that tell you?"
  (high iron oxide — well-drained soil; and it's distinctive, so a good
  comparison feature)

### 0:36–0:56 (20 min) — Direct Teaching + Station: The Full IAFIS Patterns

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "Last year: arch, loop, whorl. The real classification has **eight**:

  | Family | Types |
  |---|---|
  | **Arch** | plain arch, tented arch |
  | **Loop** | radial loop (opens toward the thumb), ulnar loop (opens toward the pinky) |
  | **Whorl** | plain whorl, central pocket loop whorl, double loop whorl, accidental whorl |

  - **Plain whorl** — complete circles, two deltas.
  - **Central pocket loop whorl** — a loop with a little whorl/pocket at the
    center.
  - **Double loop whorl** — two loops swirled together (an S or yin-yang shape).
  - **Accidental whorl** — a mix of two pattern types, or something that fits
    none.

  **Delta counts:** arch 0, loop 1, whorl 2. **Frequencies:** loops ~60–65%,
  whorls ~30–35%, arches ~5%.

  **Minutiae** (the little details you match): ridge ending, **bifurcation**
  (a ridge splits), dot, island/short ridge, lake/enclosure, spur, bridge,
  crossover, plus the **delta** and **core**.

  **Three kinds of prints at a scene:**
  - **Latent** — invisible; left by sweat and skin oils. You have to **develop**
    it: dusting powder, **superglue (cyanoacrylate) fuming** → white, **ninhydrin**
    → purple (reacts with amino acids), iodine fuming, or fluorescent dye under a
    special light.
  - **Patent** — visible, because the finger was coated in something —
    **blood, ink, paint, grease, dust** — and transferred a print. Just
    photograph it.
  - **Plastic / impressed** — a 3-D mold, the finger pressed into something soft:
    **putty, wax, soft clay, tacky paint**. Photograph with side lighting; cast it.

  **Vocabulary one-pointers:**
  - **Dermatoglyphics** — the study of friction-ridge skin patterns.
  - **Dactyloscopy** — fingerprint identification / comparison.
  - Ridges form in the **dermal papillae — the papillary layer of the dermis** —
    before birth, and never change.
  - **Adermatoglyphia** — a rare inherited condition: born with **no
    fingerprints**.
  - **Hexadactyly** (polydactyly) — born with a **sixth finger or toe**.
  - **AFIS** = Automated Fingerprint Identification System; **IAFIS** = Integrated
    AFIS (FBI); **NCIC** = National Crime Information Center; **CODIS** = Combined
    DNA Index System (a *DNA* database — the trick is it's not 'Combined Official
    DNA Identification System')."

- **[ACTIVITY: Fingerprint Classification Station]**
  Each kid inks (or graphite-and-tapes) all ten of their own fingers onto a card,
  then uses the IAFIS reference chart to classify each print into one of the eight
  patterns, and counts deltas. Compare across the team — how many loops vs.
  whorls vs. arches? Does it roughly match the population percentages?

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "A print has two loops swirled around each other like a
  yin-yang. Which of the eight?" (double loop whorl) "You dust a latent print
  with powder — what type of print was it before you dusted?" (latent —
  invisible) "What does CODIS stand for?" (Combined DNA Index System)

### 0:56–1:10 (14 min) — Shoeprints: Class vs. Individual

- **COACH SCRIPT:**
  "A shoeprint has **class characteristics** — size, overall tread design, brand,
  general wear pattern — that put it in a big group. And **individual
  characteristics** — a specific cut, a gouge, a worn-through spot, a stuck
  pebble — that can tie it to **one exact shoe**.

  **2-D prints** are on hard surfaces (dust, a wet track on tile). **3-D prints**
  are pressed into soil, sand, or snow — those can be **cast** with dental stone.

  Direction and gait: the print shows which way the toe pushed off; a longer
  **stride** (heel of one foot to the next print of the *same* foot) and a deep
  toe impression suggest running. Tires: an arrowhead tread points the way the
  drive wheels turn; long skid marks mean higher speed before stopping. Per the
  rules, **no calculations** are expected for track direction or speed."

- **[ACTIVITY: Shoeprint Comparison]**
  Kids compare the printed crime-scene shoeprint to the suspect print. They list
  the **class characteristics** that match (size, tread pattern) and find the one
  **individual characteristic** (a specific defect) that confirms it's the same
  shoe. One sentence: "class characteristics are consistent; the [defect] is an
  individual characteristic that identifies this specific shoe."

- **[CHECKPOINT:]** "Same size and same herringbone tread — class or individual
  match? Does it prove it's the same shoe?" (class; no — lots of shoes match)
  "A triangular cut in the third lug that appears in both prints?" (individual —
  that identifies the specific shoe)

## Coach Notes & Common Kid Mistakes

- **Reading the triangle lines in the wrong direction.** Each axis reads parallel
  to one of the *other* two sides, not straight to the opposite corner. Practice
  with 3 known examples until the hand motion is automatic.
- **Forgetting the percentages sum to 100.** If given only two, the third is
  100 minus the sum.
- **Clay particle size.** It's **< 0.002 mm** — the small one. Kids reverse sand
  and clay sizes.
- **"CODIS is a fingerprint database."** No — CODIS is **DNA**. IAFIS/AFIS/NGI
  are the fingerprint systems.
- **Calling every whorl a "plain whorl."** Learn the four sub-types — double loop
  (yin-yang) and central pocket (loop with a tiny whorl core) are the ones tests
  like.
- **Latent vs. patent mixed up.** **Latent = invisible** (must be developed).
  **Patent = plain to see** (transferred in blood/ink/grease). Mnemonic:
  "patent = patently obvious."
- **Treating a same-size-same-tread match as proof.** That's class evidence. You
  need an individual characteristic (a specific defect) to identify the one shoe.
